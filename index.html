Vehicle Repair Tracking System (VRTS) for SparkAuto Workshop – Interface Prototypes for Increments 1 to 3

Group: The Specialists · Waterfall Campus

Screens built around the people who use them: the manager, the front desk and the mechanics.

Designed around real people. SparkAuto is a family workshop, and the people using the VRTS are busy: a manager juggling jobs, a front-desk clerk talking to customers, and mechanics with oily hands. So every screen speaks plainly, tells you what is happening, and helps you avoid mistakes before they happen. The six prototypes below (two per increment) share one layout, so nobody has to learn the system twice. Each one names its increment and functional requirement, explains why it exists, and shows how Nielsen's usability heuristics were applied.

Prototype 1: Login screen

Increment 1: Foundation and Core AdministrationFR-001 Secure login👤 Used by: all staff

Purpose of the screen

This is the entry point to the VRTS. It lets only authorised workshop staff (manager, administrative staff and mechanics) sign in with a username and password, and it sends each role to the correct home screen. It replaces the open paper job-card cabinet with controlled access.

Usability heuristics applied

  • Visibility of system status: a "Signing in…" state on the button and a secure-connection note in the footer.
  • Error prevention / help with errors: the error message says what failed and how many attempts remain; the password can be revealed to avoid typing mistakes.
  • Consistency and standards: familiar username, password and primary button pattern.
  • User control: "Forgot password?" gives an exit route.
  • Aesthetic and minimalist design: only the fields needed to sign in.

Prototype 2: Service jobs and mechanic assignment

Increment 1: Foundation and Core AdministrationFR-002 Manage service jobsFR-005 Assign mechanic👤 Used by: workshop manager and front desk

Purpose of the screen

This is the digital replacement for the paper job card. Staff can search, create, view, edit and delete service jobs with vehicle and owner details. The workshop manager assigns a mechanic to each job from the same list, so no separate screen is needed.

Usability heuristics applied

  • Visibility of system status: colour-coded status pills and a confirmation banner after every save or assignment.
  • Match with the real world: "Job card", "Awaiting Parts" and "Ready for Collection" use workshop language.
  • Recognition rather than recall: the mechanic drop-down shows current workload, so the manager does not need to remember it.
  • Error prevention: delete opens a confirmation dialog, and the Delete button is hidden for mechanics.
  • Flexibility and efficiency: search and status filters above the table.

Prototype 3: Spare parts inventory and parts on a job

Increment 2: Operational Workflow and Safety ControlsFR-003 Manage spare partsFR-004 Link parts to a job👤 Used by: workshop manager and mechanics

Purpose of the screen

The left side is the parts catalogue, where the manager creates, edits and deletes part records with name, price and stock. The right side lets staff add a part to a specific job. The stock level drops automatically and the part is billed to that job.

Usability heuristics applied

  • Visibility of system status: stock updates immediately, and low-stock rows carry a red "Low" pill.
  • Error prevention: quantity cannot exceed stock on hand, and the panel warns before stock reaches zero.
  • Recognition rather than recall: the part is chosen from a searchable list with price and stock beside it.
  • Consistency: same table, buttons and pills as the jobs screen.
  • User control: a "Remove" link lets a part be taken off the job, which returns the stock.

Prototype 4: Mechanic job screen on a cellphone (repair progress and safety checks)

Increment 2: Operational Workflow and Safety ControlsFR-006 Track repair progressFR-009 Prevent unsafe release👤 Used by: mechanics

Purpose of the screen

A cellphone screen used on the workshop floor (the other five screens are desktop screens). The mechanic updates the repair status with large tap buttons and completes a safety checklist. "Ready for Collection" stays disabled until every check is done and all required parts are assigned. This stops a car being released with a misread note, which is the problem that started the project.

Usability heuristics applied

  • Visibility of system status: a progress count (3 of 5 checks) and a clear message that explains why release is blocked.
  • Error prevention: the release button is disabled, not just warned about, until FR-009 rules are met.
  • Recognition rather than recall: checklist items are listed, so mechanics do not rely on memory or handwriting.
  • Flexibility and usability for casual users: one-column layout, big thumb-sized buttons and few words, so training takes under 20 minutes.
  • User control: a checked item can be unticked, and status can be moved back.

Prototype 5: Job cost and digital invoice

Increment 3: Financial Calculations and Management ReportingFR-007 Calculate repair costFR-008 Generate invoice👤 Used by: front desk

Purpose of the screen

Once a job is complete, the system adds labour and parts automatically and shows a printable invoice. Staff check the figures and print or export it as a PDF. This removes hand calculation errors and speeds up customer handover. Only the manager can edit the labour rate.

Usability heuristics applied

  • Visibility of system status: a "Job complete – all checks passed" banner shows the invoice is safe to issue.
  • Match with the real world: the layout follows a normal workshop invoice.
  • Error prevention: totals are calculated by the system and not typed in; the rate field is locked for non-managers.
  • Consistency: the same navigation, buttons and colours as earlier screens.
  • User control and efficiency: Print, Export PDF and Back to jobs are always visible.

Prototype 6: Management reports

Increment 3: Financial Calculations and Management ReportingFR-010 Weekly repair and low-stock reports👤 Used by: workshop manager

Purpose of the screen

Gives the workshop manager a weekly summary of completed repairs and a list of parts that are running low, produced on demand. It helps the manager decide what to re-order and how busy the workshop has been. The system only flags low stock and does not order from suppliers.

Usability heuristics applied

  • Visibility of system status: the report shows its date range and when it was generated.
  • Recognition rather than recall: key figures sit in summary tiles above a simple chart and table.
  • Flexibility and efficiency: the week can be changed, and results can be exported.
  • Error prevention: a date range check blocks an end date before the start date.
  • Aesthetic and minimalist design: one chart and one table, with no extra decoration.
